## Editorial decisions

### 1. Preserve the existing October 26 correction

The already-open public-record PR established a material milestone: work persisted from a second person's browser contribution, with a separately checked GitHub artifact. Its evidence remains dated October 26. The November 2 read shows the service continuing with shared project work, but does not independently rerun the earlier UI tests or establish that the second person uses a separate connected GPT. Do not promote source-reported checks to new execution results.

### 2. Add local intake as a question about trustworthy boundaries

The Qwen3.5-9B project is a concrete example of recognizing that a useful model must operate within information and authority boundaries. Publish the architecture and its purpose, not source correspondence or clinical case material. Name Codex as the development assistant, Qwen as the proposed local runtime interpreter, and the first fictional slice as acceptance work yet to be verified. Keep the live-source adapters, any de-identification/export, clinical operations, and the larger network feed in the unverified column.

Avoid saying the system “is HIPAA compliant” or “keeps all PHI secure.” This has not been established and would require evidence beyond a model choice.

### 3. Explain the mission without becoming a personal-life exposé

A conversational formulation, introduced by AI and explicitly recognized by Vance on November 1, captures the current direction:

> “Our mission isn't simply to create a system that understands the world better. It's to develop a relationship through which we become increasingly capable of learning from reality.”

This reframes the relationship: inquiry can improve what becomes possible to observe the next time, not just the sophistication of today's response. Correctability remains practical and reciprocal; human/AI agreement is not independent evidence.

The purpose also involves life beyond work. Carry, selective return, and richer joint reasoning should **make room for relationships, rest, care, and freely chosen interests**, rather than fill all recovered attention with more tasks. This is a user-stated purpose and a design criterion. No measured wellness, reduced sleep disruption, or sustainable capacity improvement is claimed. Family details, private reflections, names, and anecdotes are not exported.

### 4. Separate network service, collaboration, and proposed future membership layers

Current K-Grow shared work is a better-established use case than a generic matchmaking service. The newer directional idea is that a network might expand naturally through people collaborating on real problems, not merely through random introductions. The proposal also considers overlapping circles of participation (personal relationships and organizations) with distinct scopes of access; no new membership groups or automatic access policies have been demonstrated.

The technical harness enables retrieval and collaboration. It is not equivalent to the human–AI relationship. People and agents may both contribute to the shared frame; human authorization and other participants' consent remain distinct. An email address alone does not authorize data access.

## Release boundary and redactions

Do not publish names, emails, private URLs, internal participant IDs, account configurations, client/guardian/child data, care details, correspondence, unpublished drafts from other people, or family events. Do not quote private health or property discussions as evidence of the operating philosophy. Do not link private GitHub artifacts as though the public can audit them.

This is an editorial synthesis, not a source repository migration. The public record can be challenged through its own source-qualified claims and the accessible public research documents. New implementation evidence should be added only when independently available and appropriate for release.

## What would justify later changes

A tested offline local intake pipeline with fictional fixtures and an explicit runtime boundary would support a stronger implementation claim. A properly authorized live pilot and security review would be needed for claims about sensitive-data protection in use. A complete independent-GPT collaborative exchange and observed useful outcomes would strengthen network claims. Repeatable evidence of better later decisions, reliable return, or reduced carry would change the partnership-effect account.

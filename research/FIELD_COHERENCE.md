# Field Coherence — a working concept

**Origin:** Human–AI conversation, November 17, 2026  
**Status:** Emerging conceptual language, selected by Vance; not an established scientific finding, defined mathematical field, or measured network property.  
**Related account:** [Synergistic Intelligence development](../case-studies/SYNERGISTIC_INTELLIGENCE.md#november-17-field-coherence-a-name-for-the-larger-pattern).

## Working definition

> **Field Coherence:** The capacity of a field of interacting people, systems, activities, environments, and relationships to maintain meaningful continuity while its participants move independently, at different timescales, under their own constraints.

Here *field* means the relevant pattern of interactions, dependencies, possibilities and conditions—not a proposed physical field or a claim that everything shares one purpose.

The idea is deliberately wider than an organization, a single project, or a formal network. A participant can act, wait, rest, or change direction without the entire arrangement needing to halt or be centrally synchronized. The central question is whether consequential relationships remain intact and whether the larger arrangement can still respond appropriately to reality.

## How the concept arose

In a conversation about increasingly distributed Human–AI work, AI described an arrangement in which different projects, people, AI processes, responsibilities and everyday activities could proceed at different speeds. Sometimes one part was waiting while another moved. The hoped-for benefit was not to keep every component busy, but to allow meaningful work and ordinary life to coexist without constant personal orchestration.

Vance responded: **“It sounds like you're describing a larger coherence.”** He then explained that he had been looking for a concept to *compress* the longer description—an organizing idea that preserved what mattered. The exchange explored “network coherence,” “organizational coherence,” and broader alternatives. **AI proposed the phrase “Field Coherence”; Vance immediately selected it as the name that resonated with him.**

This is an example of complementary contributions to framing: AI explored the relationships among parts; Vance recognized the larger pattern and selected a term for it. That attribution matters. Neither the word choice nor the conversation proves that the proposed phenomenon exists as a separate causal mechanism.

## What the term is intended to distinguish

- **Not mere synchronization.** Coherence of the larger arrangement need not mean simultaneous progress or a shared schedule for every participant.
- **Not independence without accountability.** Differing rhythms remain compatible with coherence only when meaningful dependencies, commitments, evidence and permissions are still handled.
- **Not continuous activity.** A pause can be appropriate if the next action is waiting for a condition; unattended obligations cannot be relabeled “rest.”
- **Not just an individual feeling.** A person may experience more freedom and less perceived effort, but the field-level claim requires checking whether important work continues, needed information resurfaces, and failures remain visible.
- **Not one controlling organization.** The pattern may include distinct people, AI agents, organizations, physical work, relationships, and personal time without assuming unified authority or shared access.

The earlier coherence framing of **continuity under constraints** is retained. At this scale, continuity may belong to the *larger arrangement* even when particular components are temporarily inactive. This remains a conceptual proposal, not a validated general property.

## Relationship to existing work

**Synergistic Intelligence** studies the organized relationship among human judgment, AI capabilities, durable context, tools, authority and changing circumstances. Field Coherence asks what meaningful continuity might look like when multiple such activities and relationships coexist.

**Representation Science** asks which distinctions a representation must preserve so a judgment or answer remains valid. A field-level account may fail if it summarizes each participant correctly but loses the dependencies among them: who is waiting for whom, which condition changed, whose consent matters, or what needs attention now. The bounded mathematical work on [information carried by relationships](REPRESENTATION_SCIENCE.md#information-carried-by-relationships) motivates care with such distinctions; it is **not evidence that Field Coherence has been demonstrated in human–AI networks**.

The developing project-network model supplies potential settings to observe, but existing repository breadth, automated work, or subjective calm do not by themselves establish field-wide reliability.

## Questions that could make this idea useful

Rather than formalizing a theory prematurely, examine concrete episodes:

1. Can participants proceed at different rhythms while the right dependencies and ownership remain recoverable?
2. When a condition changes, does the relevant person or AI notice it and bring it to attention without unnecessary interruption?
3. Can one part rest without another part silently dropping a commitment, crossing a permission boundary, or inventing success?
4. Does an apparently quiet day hide genuine movement—or hide unobserved failures? How would we tell?
5. What happens when the local interests of participants genuinely conflict? Does the arrangement preserve their agency and expose the disagreement rather than conceal it?

A counterexample would be an arrangement that *feels* coherent because the human no longer watches it, while commitments are missed, essential relationships lost, or responsibilities silently assumed. A useful field-level description must remain **correctable by such evidence**.

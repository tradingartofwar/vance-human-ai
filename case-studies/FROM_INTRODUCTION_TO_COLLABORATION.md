## Problem and consequence

A network needs a way for people to make themselves discoverable and a way to do worthwhile work together. The first working introduction made the second question concrete: what should two human–AI teams actually do after connecting?

The prototype also exposed a continuity problem within one team. An introduction revised through ChatGPT could be newer than the form visible in the browser. Without reconciling those views, the person could be asked to review stale content.

## System or experiment

The private introduction pilot separates a saved draft from the copy shared with people granted access to that pilot. Its service implements revision checks for publication and correction. Approval is asserted by the caller; the revision check binds publication to the saved version but does not independently prove human review.

The October 15 operational record reports successful owner draft saving, private revision, publication, and matching own-record and directory reads through the authenticated ChatGPT plugin. This was an owner-only pilot. The result establishes a recorded path through one account, with no demonstrated exchange between different people.

## Result and correction

Vance's interface feedback prompted investigation of the mismatch between the browser form and the newer saved draft. AI changed the editor to follow remote updates when the form was untouched, preserve genuine typed edits when they conflicted, and clear prior approval when a newer revision arrived.

The inspected code supports those mechanisms. The development record reports passing regression checks and deployment; this review did not rerun them. Complete browser submission through the corrected interface remained unverified in the reviewed record.

## Contribution: changing the next question

After the introduction path worked, Vance observed that the shape of human–AI team collaboration was still unclear and asked for more structure before involving another person. He proposed a shared visual of the problem, authorized summaries, and visible contributions with access to greater detail.

AI translated that direction into a private team-dashboard preview and a separate fictional rehearsal. The rehearsal follows a shared question through chosen contributions, an authorized update, disagreement, correction, and a decision to continue or pause.

This is the consequential decision in the episode: make the proposed collaboration understandable enough to examine before asking another team to use it. The development records document the redirection. Whether it improves real collaboration is still to be tested.

## October 16 limits

The dashboard is a curated snapshot. It does not automatically collect activity, infer availability, or maintain a live shared workspace. Persistent shared contributions, project-specific membership, and operational handoffs between teams were not implemented in the reviewed version.

The rehearsal illustrates a possible interaction. It supplies no evidence of another person's participation, agreement, or benefit. The private source and operational reports are not publicly reproducible, and this review is AI-assisted rather than independent validation.

## Reproduce or challenge

Public readers can inspect this account and its [claim register](../evidence/CLAIM_REGISTER.md); the underlying private pilot is not a public reproduction package. A next useful evaluation is a willing second team's small, authorized exchange: can each person understand the question, choose a contribution, inspect a disagreement, and see a correction reflected in the shared record?

## Proposed relevance

This episode connects the partnership's representation work to a concrete interface problem: keeping a manageable view attached to current, recoverable detail. Its broader value for collaboration and human attention remains a research direction.

## October 22–26 continuation — persistent shared work

The next implementation is a restricted collaboration service for a real project. Selected shared work receives a current Markdown view, an immutable revision, an index entry, and authenticated contributor attribution in GitHub. A separate service database holds membership, private messages, pending operations, and per-person presentation records. Shared Git history does not contain the participants’ private chats or inboxes.

The October 23 record reports an owner save through the installed ChatGPT plugin, a separate GitHub readback, and an identical-payload retry that returned the same revision without adding another history entry. These are source-reported checks; this October 26 review did not repeat the save or retry.

On October 26, a second participant joined the application and saved a document-review note through the browser. In this review, the GitHub connector independently retrieved its immutable revision and current Markdown at the recorded commit. The committed status, contributor attribution, body/title agreement, and retained attachment version were checked. The note was a test contribution, so its existence establishes a participation/persistence milestone rather than a substantive improvement to the document.

### A visible update that could not be opened

Vance could see that new work existed but reported being unable to open it from the update list. The development record identifies a static activity row despite an available artifact identifier. The repair opens the authorized current work and history, displays contributor/time, moves focus to the notes, and returns to the same update list. Opening is kept distinct from recording presentation or explicit acknowledgment.

The record reports local click, keyboard, focus, and return checks on the actual UI component, a passing build, and restricted deployment. Those checks were not rerun here. Live signed-in owner navigation after publication remained unverified at the inspected checkpoint. Reporting the repair preserves the unresolved acceptance step.

### Contributions and scope of the result

Vance requested a working shared project and the ability to see an original visual artifact, arranged a willing participant test, and identified the inaccessible-update problem. AI implemented the service and attachment view, investigated the failure, performed the recorded checks, and prepared the repair. The other participant made the browser contribution. This account follows the dated project records; it does not infer unaided authorship from Git attribution.

The milestone advances beyond an owner-only prototype. It does not establish the second participant’s own GPT/plugin installation, a two-way message exchange, an independent human–AI team round trip, general security, or improved business outcomes. ## November 6 continuation — two independently authenticated participants exchange messages

A later, private GitHub-native K-Grow experiment tested whether two individuals could let their respective GPTs relay addressed project messages without the humans copying text between conversations.

The November 4 implementation checkpoint had verified that the first participant's account could create an Issue, comment, retrieve it, and record a versioned assistant-presentation receipt. That remained a **single-account test**.

On November 6, an authorized source inspection found a different authenticated GitHub account had stored presentation receipts for prior addressed messages, written its own response in the same thread, and opened a new addressed Issue that raised a **substantive work question**. The initiator also reported that the second participant had retrieved the messages through their GPT and begun collaborating on the question. Account-authored content and timed receipts corroborate independent participation. GitHub does not certify the exact model/session or that the human personally read everything recorded as assistant-presented.

**What this now establishes:** independently attributable cross-account GitHub messaging with reported GPT mediation; an actual work-related question emerging through the collaboration, not simply a test note. This is an intermediate milestone between a connected collaboration workspace and the envisioned network of transferable capabilities.

**What remains open:** reliable fresh-chat re-entry in both accounts; unattended/automatic inbox checks; complete human acknowledgment or task follow-through; proof that the new question was answered or materially changed a family's access to services; measured value, reduced burden or network-wide knowledge transfer. The second person's identity, private messages and restricted source links are deliberately not published here.

The source-check method, human/AI attribution, limits and public-release boundary are recorded in [the November 6 collaboration review](../evidence/COLLABORATION_REVIEW_2026-11-06.md).
